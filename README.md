# Dividend Signaling: Project Angles

**Question:** Do dividend changes signal future earnings, and does the market price that signal correctly?

**Theory:** Lintner (1956); Bhattacharya (1979); Miller & Rock (1985).

**Method:** event studies per Fama, Fisher, Jensen & Roll (1969) and Brown & Warner (1980); firm-clustered standard errors per Petersen (2009).

## 1. Price reaction and time horizons
How strong is the price reaction, and does it keep drifting over 3, 6 and 12 months?
- Is the response linear or does it flatten for large changes?
- Does the price move start before the announcement (leakage or anticipation in months −3 to −1)?
- At which horizon is the dividend change most informative about returns?
- Does the drift hold up after adjusting for risk with the Fama-French factors?

*Papers:* Pettit (1972); Aharony & Swary (1980); Brown & Warner (1980); Michaely, Thaler & Womack (1995); Fama & French (2015).

## 2. Future earnings
Does the signal come true: do earnings actually grow afterwards?
- Do dividend changes predict earnings growth 1, 2 and 3 years ahead, or do they mostly reflect past earnings?
- Does the price reaction get larger when earnings later do grow, i.e. does the market tell true signals apart from false ones?
- Do dividend changes add information beyond the current year's earnings change?
- Do firms that raise dividends become less risky afterwards (lower beta), as the maturity hypothesis says?

*Papers:* Kane, Lee & Marcus (1984); Benartzi, Michaely & Thaler (1997); Nissim & Ziv (2001); Grullon, Michaely & Swaminathan (2002); Grullon, Michaely, Benartzi & Thaler (2005); Ham, Kaplan & Leary (2020).

## 3. Cuts vs. raises
Do cuts move prices more than raises?
- Compare the impact of cuts vs raises on the price movement.
- Are small cuts already punished heavily, i.e. is there a jump at zero?
- Do cuts and raises differ in how long the price keeps drifting afterwards?
- Are cuts during recessions (e.g. 2008–09, 2020) punished less, because investors blame the economy rather than the firm?

*Papers:* Lintner (1956); Aharony & Swary (1980); Benartzi, Michaely & Thaler (1997); Grullon, Michaely & Swaminathan (2002).

## 4. Initiations and omissions
Do starting or stopping a dividend cause bigger reactions?
- Compare the announcement reaction to that of ordinary raises and cuts.
- Do the stocks keep drifting for 12 months after the event?
- Do earnings improve after initiations and recover after omissions?
- How long after an omission do firms start paying again, and how does the market react to the restart?

*Papers:* Michaely, Thaler & Womack (1995); Benartzi, Michaely & Thaler (1997).

## 5. Where the signal is strongest
Is the reaction larger where investors know less about the firm?
- Small vs. large firms (market cap).
- High vs. low return volatility.
- Growth vs. value firms (book-to-market from Compustat).
- First-time raisers vs. firms that raise their dividend every year: is a routine raise still news?
- Industry differences, for example utilities vs. technology.

*Papers:* Bhattacharya (1979); Miller & Rock (1985); Grullon, Michaely & Swaminathan (2002); Fama & French (2015).
